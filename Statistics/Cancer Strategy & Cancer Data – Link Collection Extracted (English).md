# Part A – Cancer control strategies and programmes (Finland & EU)

## 1. National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035 "Together against cancer – people-centred cancer prevention and care" (STM PDF)

Finland's first national cancer strategy, commissioned by the Ministry of Social Affairs and Health (STM) and drafted by FICAN; published 10 Nov 2025. Built by 9 working groups with 200+ professionals, researchers, NGO reps and citizens. Values guiding the goals: reducing inequality, quality of life, effectiveness of care, research.

**Cancer burden (from the strategy):** 38,000 new cases in 2022 → 45,000 by 2035 (+20%); 356,000 people living with a cancer diagnosis in 2022 → 480,000 (+35%); growth strongest among the elderly; costs rising fast.

**Four main goals and sub-goals:**

1. Strengthen involvement and develop people-centred services – 1.1 patients/families co-develop services; 1.2 respectful, individual encounters; 1.3 clear health communication and health literacy.
2. Reduce the cancer burden through prevention and early detection – 2.1 policy measures on risk factors; 2.2 prevention in wellbeing services counties and municipalities; 2.3 screening follows European recommendations; 2.4 legislation + registry data enable risk-based screening; 2.5 early detection across the health system.
3. Ensure equal and effective cancer care – 3.1 national treatment recommendations and care pathways; 3.2 national quality register, outcome and peer review; 3.3 equal access to rehabilitation, psychosocial support, health social work, palliative care; 3.4 functional-capacity assessment for older patients; 3.5 enough competent staff.
4. Stay on the cutting edge – 4.1 national health data + analytics; 4.2 clinical research part of every patient's care; 4.3 pioneer in precision medicine; 4.4 multidisciplinary research; 4.5 fast, consistent evaluation of new cancer drugs.

**Quantitative targets / indicators:**

- Patient experience NPS > 80 in units treating cancer; PROMs measured systematically
- 1,000 fewer preventable cancers per year
- 70% of cancers detected at stage I–II (by 2035)
- 10% reduction in cancer mortality
- Treatment within nationally defined time limits
- 5-year survival for poor-prognosis cancers at Nordic level
- > 10% of cancer patients in interventional trials
- > 70% of clinical cancer data nationally integrated by 2030
- Cancer medicines evaluated through a single channel; precision-medicine programme and national cancer research hub
- Screening: ≥80% participation in every programme in every county; breast screening age range widened to 46–74

**Investment:** \~€5.5 per person per year 2026–2030 (≈0.1% of health-care costs, ≈1.6% of expected cancer costs); estimated savings \~€305 m. Equity is a cross-cutting theme; a "right to be forgotten" law for cancer survivors is on the action list.

## 2. Implementation roadmap 2026–2031 (STM PDF)

Published 23 Sep 2026; covers the first five-year period. Describes measures, timetables, responsible actors and resources, and clarifies the roles of ministries, authorities, wellbeing services counties and NGOs. Coordinated by FICAN; steering group chaired by Hanne Kuitunen (OYS Cancer Center) with STM, THL, Cancer Society of Finland and FICAN.

- Total extra investment needed ≈ €139–140 m (FICAN asks the next government to reserve €139 m for 2027–2031); expected to pay back via prevented cases, earlier detection and more R&D.
- FICAN estimates at least \~€298 m savings over the strategy period; without action costs grow from \~€1.4 bn (2023) to up to €2.2 bn (2035).
- A monitoring group will coordinate and follow implementation; roadmap updated as needed; broad review + roadmap for the second period in 2030.
- Includes cancer-specific care pathways with Nordic-style symptom/referral criteria and target waiting times; THL, the Cancer Registry and FICAN are piloting cancer-specific quality monitoring.
- Cancer organisations note that no funding has yet been earmarked.

## 3. FICAN's cancer strategy page

Summarises the strategy (four goals, values, 200+ experts; steering group STM, THL, Cancer Society, FICAN) and links the roadmap page, the STM press release and an English summary presentation. Coordination of implementation sits with FICAN at first, later moving to the planned Cancer Mission Hub Finland.

## 4. Europe's Beating Cancer Plan (European Commission)

Launched 3 Feb 2021; aim: equal access to prevention, diagnosis, treatment and medicines wherever people live. Four action areas: prevention, early detection, equal access to diagnosis/treatment, quality of life. Funded via EU4Health, Horizon Europe, Digital Europe.

- EU 2024: 2.7 m diagnoses, 1.3 m deaths; cases projected +24% by 2035 without action; \~40% of cancers preventable.
- Inequality: breast-screening uptake ranges 6–90% and cervical 25–80% between countries; 5-year colon survival 49–68%; >12 m survivors.
- Targets: tobacco use <5% by 2040; ≥10% relative cut in harmful alcohol use; 90% of girls HPV-vaccinated by 2030; screening offered to 90% of eligible people (breast, cervical, colorectal; lung, prostate, gastric under study); 90% of eligible patients with access to Comprehensive Cancer Centres by 2030.
- 10 flagships incl. Knowledge Centre on Cancer, European Cancer Imaging Initiative (>100,000 anonymised cases and ≥50 AI models by Dec 2026), UNCAN.eu, **Cancer Inequalities Registry** (29 country cancer profiles), EU Network of Comprehensive Cancer Centres, Helping Children with Cancer. Last reviewed Feb 2025.

## 5. EU Mission on Cancer (Horizon Europe)

Goal: improve the lives of more than 3 million people by 2030 through prevention, cure and support. Four objectives: understanding cancer, prevention & early detection, diagnosis & treatment, quality of life. Cross-cutting: citizen engagement, equitable access, childhood cancer, innovation, personalised medicine. Europe: 2.7 m diagnoses and 1.3 m deaths a year; >3.24 m diagnoses expected by 2040. Key documents: implementation plan (2021), factsheet (2025). Funding calls run via Horizon Europe (2026–27 work programme).

# Part B – Cancer data in Finland: sources and websites

## 6. Finnish Cancer Registry – statistics (syoparekisteri.fi/tilastot)

Cancer statistics app + screening statistics. Page blocked; key figures from the registry's Cancer 2024 report: **38,853 new cancers, 13,490 cancer deaths, 343,041 people alive with a past cancer** in 2024. Most common cancers: prostate, breast, colorectal; colorectal cases fell to 4,153 (from 4,582) after screening began. Stage information recorded for only **42%** of cases nationally – best in the Inner-Finland collaborative area (60%) and Pirkanmaa (72%); goal ≥70%. Forecast \~48,000 new cases in 2045 (+24% vs 2024); cancers in people over 85 to more than double to \~10,000.

**Screening 2024:** breast 81.6%, colorectal 73.5%, cervical 73.5%; 2,360 cancers and 5,272 precursors found. Highest participation: South Ostrobothnia, South Savo, Kainuu; lowest: Helsinki, Vantaa–Kerava, Western Uusimaa (below 68%); gap 7–14 percentage points between counties. Reports break down participation by education and mother tongue.

## 7. Finnish Cancer Registry – front page

Research institute of the Cancer Society of Finland that maintains THL's statutory cancer register (all cases since 1953; reporting mandatory since 1961) plus the national screening registers; publishes reports and screening reports and releases data for research.

## 8. Findata – Social and Health Data Permit Authority

One-stop permit authority (since 2019–20) for secondary use of social and health data when data come from several public controllers, the private sector or Kanta. After a permit, Findata combines and pseudonymises data and delivers it to a secure environment (Kapseli®).

- Typical processing: permit 2–4 months; data delivery 3–6 months; statistical data request 3–6 months.
- 2026 (to 10 Nov): 108 data-permit applications, 162 amendments, 15 statistics requests, 265 decisions (87% positive). Totals: 916 permit applications, 2,050 decisions.
- New e-service launches 11 Jan 2027.

## 9. THL – Data and statistics + Sotkanet indicator bank

THL data page blocked. **Sotkanet** (THL, CC BY 4.0) holds 3,600+ indicators on population health, welfare and services, by municipality, wellbeing services county and whole country, with tables, charts and maps. Cancer-related indicators found include:

- (EU) age-standardised cancer mortality per 100,000 (ind. 10024)
- Specialist outpatient visits, cancer & radiotherapy (2475)
- People entitled to special-reimbursed cancer medicines, % of population and age-standardised (5395, 5093)
- 5-year relative survival: breast (3625), cervical (3626), colorectal (3627)
- Screening participation: mammography (3621), Pap (3620), colorectal (6103)
- Cancer index, not age-standardised (5644)

These county-level indicators are directly usable for regional equity comparisons.

## 10. Statistics Finland – Causes of death (StatFin)

2025: neoplasms caused **13,832 deaths** (7,463 men / 6,369 women), almost a quarter of all deaths; median age 77. Age-standardised cancer mortality down >8% in 10 years (men −12%, women −8%); men's rate \~1.5× women's.

- Lung: 2,302 deaths; pancreas 1,391 (second most common fatal cancer); breast 900; prostate 1,017.
- Working-age (15–64): \~2,000 cancer deaths, almost a third of working-age deaths.
- StatFin table **13u5** gives deaths by cause by wellbeing services county (updated 29 Oct 2026). 2024: 13,722 neoplasm deaths.

## 11. Kela – Info Tray (Tietotarjotin) open data + reimbursement entitlements (avoindata)

Tietotarjotin open-data page was under maintenance. Dataset **"Existing, new and withdrawn entitlements to reimbursement of medicine expenses"** (Kelasto): annual data from 1986, by wellbeing services county, age and sex; counts entitlements started, ended at death, valid during and at end of year. Covers special-reimbursement diseases (incl. cancer), restricted special/basic reimbursement medicines and clinical nutrients. Updated yearly by \~20 Feb; contact tilastot@kela.fi. Useful as a proxy for cancer prevalence by region.

## 12. Kela & Fimea – Finnish Statistics on Medicines 2025

Joint annual publication (since 1987; CC BY 4.0) on medicine consumption and reimbursements. 2025: total medicine sales €4.2 bn (+4%, +€165 m), of which outpatient prescription €2,924 m and hospital sales €873 m; customers bought reimbursable medicines for €2.74 bn; Kela reimbursed €1.98 bn (+4%). Sales of haematological medicines fell despite slightly higher use. Kela/Fimea also offer apps for medicine purchases by ATC class and by wellbeing services county.

## 13. FICAN – Cancer registry and quality registers

FICAN, THL and the Cancer Registry run a **cancer quality register pilot (2024–2027)** testing indicators such as access to care, care delivered and outcomes on selected patient groups. Reports published for lung (2018–2022 and 2019–2023), prostate, myeloma, colorectal and breast cancer. Unlike other Nordic countries, Finland has no unified cancer quality register yet.

## 14. FICAN – Finnish Cancer Center

Established under the centralisation decree to plan and coordinate cancer prevention, diagnostics, treatment and rehabilitation nationally and secure the research base. A network of wellbeing services counties, HUS and medical universities with five regional centres – **FICAN South (Helsinki), East (Kuopio), West (Turku), North (Oulu), Mid (Tampere)** – plus a national coordinating unit.

## 15. iCAN Flagship Project

Research Council of Finland flagship (University of Helsinki + HUS). Biobank study combining deep molecular tumour profiling with longitudinal health data. Aim: profile **10,000 patient samples** pan-cancer with the five FICAN centres and six biobanks; >3,000 participants already, >40 indications (colorectal, gastric, pancreatic, breast, lung, ovarian, renal, prostate, bladder, melanoma, paediatric solid tumours, leukaemias, lymphomas, myeloma). Public–private consortium; analysis on the iCAN Discovery Platform.

## 16. FinRegistry

THL/FIMM research project linking national registries for everyone living in Finland on 1 Jan 2010 plus parents, spouses, children and siblings: **\~7.2 million people** (5.34 m index persons + 1.83 m relatives); data updated to 2025; >3,000 clinical endpoints (FinnGen definitions); cancer data since 1953. Endpoints explorable at risteys.finregistry.fi.

## 17. Cancer Society of Finland (Syöpäjärjestöt)

Umbrella of the Cancer Society and the Cancer Foundation: \~4,500 volunteers; \~97,000 members in regional associations and patient organisations; €7.7 m in research grants per year (Cancer Foundation >€5 m/year – the largest private cancer-research funder in Finland). Free, low-threshold support services. Maintains the Cancer Registry.

## 18. Biobanks – Fingenious portal and FINBB

**FINBB** (Finnish Biobank Cooperative) gives centralised access to Finnish biobanks; owned by five wellbeing services counties, HUS, six universities and THL; eight member biobanks; national node of BBMRI-ERIC; partner of FinnGen; coordinates the new FinTrials clinical-trial model. **Fingenious®** is its one-stop service: browse cohorts, feasibility requests, sample/data access with one contract, and participant recruitment (consenting donors can be re-contacted). Samples and data require permission and are for research use.

## 19. Personalised Cancer Care Model Country (Mallimaa)

Project coordinated by the Finnish Cancer Institute Foundation to strengthen Finland as a pioneer in personalised cancer care and an attractive research environment. Demands: an effective implementation plan for the strategy and reform of the multi-channel drug evaluation system. Highlights on the site: almost 70% of oncologists say their unit lacks resources for onco-geriatric assessment; regional differences in cancer-care resources; Finland, unlike other Nordic countries, cannot yet systematically compare cancer-care quality between hospitals or regions.

## 20. Pharma Industry Finland (Lääketeollisuus ry) – positions

- **News, 10 Nov 2025:** supports the strategy and commits to implementation; stresses equity and timely access; says only about half of new medicines are available to Finnish patients and outcomes lag in several hard-to-treat cancers; calls for reform of new-drug evaluation, a Cancer Research Hub and adequate public investment.
- **Blog, 16 Sep 2026 (Timo Seppälä, Amgen):** proposes wellbeing services counties challenge each other with concrete health targets, using quality registers to compare results and spread best practice – turning the strategy and roadmap into regional commitments.
- **Front page:** current themes – new-medicine prices among Europe's lowest, assessment of new therapies, health sector as a growth engine.

## 21. FICAN – Cancer registry (English page)

The Cancer Registry holds all cancer cases since 1953 and is also a statistical and epidemiological research institute; runs the screening registry. Reports are mandatory for every diagnosed or strongly suspected cancer, submitted from clinical, pathology or treatment data via SFTP or e-form. Links Cancer 2022 statistics report (2022: 37,268 new cancers, 13,287 deaths).

## 22. NORDCAN 2.0 (IARC / Association of Nordic Cancer Registries)

Comparable cancer statistics for Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Faroe Islands and Greenland: fact sheets plus interactive tools for incidence and mortality trends, cohort analysis, 5-year prevalence, 5-year relative survival and incidence predictions. Best tool for Finland-vs-Nordic benchmarking.

# Supplementary context (not in the PDF, relevant to equity)

IHE Cancer Dashboard for Finland (2026) – useful benchmark data:

- Incidence 2023: 39,199 cases; 654 per 100,000 (2nd highest in Nordics). Mortality: 243 per 100,000.
- Lowest all-cancer 5-year survival in the Nordics: 69% men, 73% women.
- 61% of cases (2019–2023) with unknown stage.
- Breast screening varies 72% (Helsinki) – 86% (Åland); lower participation among less-educated women.
- HPV vaccination girls 62% (lowest in Nordics), boys 53%; county range 63–80% girls.
- Novel medicine uptake 51% (EU 54%); NGS testing 17% of biopsies vs Sweden 33%; one OECI-accredited comprehensive cancer centre.
- Cancer = 4.3% of health spending (EU 6.9%).
