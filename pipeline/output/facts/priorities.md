# Which gaps matter most: priorities for decision-makers

Every gap the project knows of is sorted by a fixed rule, not by an AI model. Three facts decide: how the gap is known and whether it is clearly larger than chance, how large it is, and how many people it affects. A gap is always a pair: a group of people and a result on the care pathway.

## 1. Identify the gap

### High relevance (6)

Measured directly, clearly larger than chance, large, and many people affected. Act on it first.

- By level of education: Cervical screening participation: 50.8 % in the group 'primary_or_unknown' against 80.3 % in 'tertiary', 31,338 people in the disadvantaged group. Pathway: detection. Measure to evaluate: 1.1.1 (Co-design screening and prevention services with residents).
- By level of education: Breast screening participation: 63.5 % in the group 'primary_or_unknown' against 85.9 % in 'tertiary', 37,116 people in the disadvantaged group. Pathway: detection. Measure to evaluate: 1.1.1 (Co-design screening and prevention services with residents).
- By native language: Breast screening participation: 60.8 % in the group 'other_or_missing' against 83.2 % in 'domestic', 24,803 people in the disadvantaged group. Pathway: detection. Measure to evaluate: 1.3.1 (Targeted community-based communication for language groups).
- By native language: Colorectal screening participation: 53.4 % in the group 'other_or_missing' against 74.5 % in 'domestic', 19,720 people in the disadvantaged group. Pathway: detection. Measure to evaluate: 1.3.1 (Targeted community-based communication for language groups).
- By native language: Cervical screening participation: 56.3 % in the group 'other_or_missing' against 76.2 % in 'domestic', 34,391 people in the disadvantaged group. Pathway: detection. Measure to evaluate: 1.3.1 (Targeted community-based communication for language groups).
- By level of education: Colorectal screening participation: 61.5 % in the group 'primary_or_unknown' against 79.3 % in 'tertiary', 68,571 people in the disadvantaged group. Pathway: detection. Measure to evaluate: 1.1.1 (Co-design screening and prevention services with residents).

### Medium relevance (33)

Measured and clearly larger than chance, but smaller, affecting fewer people, or only visible as a pattern between areas.

- By sex: Colorectal screening participation: 69.2 % in the group 'male' against 77.7 % in 'female', 205,202 people in the disadvantaged group. Pathway: detection. Measure to evaluate: 2.4.1 (Screening quality manuals in procurement and steering).
- By level of education: Colonoscopy attendance after a positive screening test: 67.2 % in the group 'primary_or_unknown' against 75.5 % in 'tertiary', 2,460 people in the disadvantaged group. Pathway: diagnosis. Measure to evaluate: 1.2.3 (Named care coordinator model).
- By region of residence: 20 wellbeing services counties are at least 5 points below the 80 % screening target in at least one programme: Central Finland, Central Ostrobothnia, Central Uusimaa, City of Helsinki, East Uusimaa, Kanta-Häme, Kymenlaakso, Lapland, North Karelia, North Ostrobothnia, North Savo, Ostrobothnia, Pirkanmaa, Päijät-Häme, Satakunta, South Karelia, South Ostrobothnia, Southwest Finland, Vantaa and Kerava, West Uusimaa.
- 2 patterns between areas, for example by employment, native language. They describe areas, not people.

### Low relevance (23) and uncertain (53)

23 gaps are measured and clearly larger than chance, but small. 53 differences could be chance or rest on a single source; most are patterns between regions for income, employment and access to primary care. They are not a basis for action on their own.

### Not measurable (13)

No open data allows a measurement. This is not the same as unimportant: the problem may be large. The first step is to make it measurable.

- Occupational health care is fast and free at the point of use; public health centres are under-resourced, so people outside working life wait longer (pathway: referral from primary to specialist care)
- Client fees, medicine and travel costs can delay seeking care or lead to skipped medicines (pathway: referral from primary to specialist care)
- Differences in health literacy and knowledge of services and rights; patients who cannot demand urgent care wait longer (pathway: referral from primary to specialist care)
- Worse outcomes beyond 50 km from the treating unit; less palliative radiotherapy beyond 100 km (pathway: after treatment has started)
- Lower screening participation among people born abroad (pathway: detection)
- In an unpublished lymphoma study cited by the roadmap, getting a diagnosis took about twice as long in public care as in private care (pathway: referral from primary to specialist care)
- Time from first primary care contact to specialist referral by region and cancer type (pathway: referral from primary to specialist care)
- Time from referral to diagnosis by region and cancer type (pathway: diagnosis)
- Time from diagnosis to treatment start and share within target time (pathway: treatment initiation)
- Stage at diagnosis by region and by education or income (pathway: detection)
- Screening participation by income group (pathway: detection)
- Date of first symptom or first contact before diagnosis (pathway: diagnosis)
- Stage at diagnosis for the 60 % of cases without a clinical notification (pathway: detection)

## 2. Locate the gap in the care pathway

The challenge names three bottlenecks. This is how the gaps are spread over them:

| Bottleneck | High | Medium | Low | Uncertain | Not measurable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Late detection | 6 | 33 | 23 | 27 | 4 |
| Transition from primary to specialist care | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 7 |
| Time from diagnosis to treatment | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| None of the three | 0 | 0 | 0 | 25 | 1 |

- Late detection is where the open data is strong: 39 gaps of high or medium relevance, all in screening participation and in the follow-up examination after a positive screening test.
- For the transition from primary to specialist care and for the time from diagnosis to treatment, no open data compares regions or groups. What exists are single figures from hospitals and one study and barriers described in strategy texts. These bottlenecks may be large; they can not be ranked.

## 3. Recommend what to do

In this order:

1. Measure 1.3.1, Targeted community-based communication for language groups: addresses 3 gaps of high and 11 of medium relevance. Cost: 400,000 euros, National estimate for 2026-2031. Success is measured by: Participation of other-language speakers reaches the level of Finnish and Swedish speakers. Source: Implementation Roadmap 2026-2031, p. 33.
2. Measure 1.1.1, Co-design screening and prevention services with residents: addresses 3 gaps of high and 7 of medium relevance. Cost: 1,500,000 euros, National estimate for 2026-2031. Success is measured by: Participation of the group with primary education. Source: Implementation Roadmap 2026-2031, p. 26.
3. Measure 2.4.1, Screening quality manuals in procurement and steering: addresses 0 gaps of high and 12 of medium relevance. Cost: 350,000 euros, National estimate for 2026-2031. Success is measured by: At least 80 % participation in every programme and every region. Source: Implementation Roadmap 2026-2031, p. 47.
4. Measure 1.2.3, Named care coordinator model: addresses 0 gaps of high and 1 of medium relevance. Cost: 100,000 euros, National estimate for 2026-2031. Success is measured by: Attendance at the follow-up examination after a positive screening result. Source: Implementation Roadmap 2026-2031, p. 32.
5. Measure 3.1.2, National care pathways with agreed access times and public monitoring: makes the two bottlenecks without data measurable. Cost: 25,000,000 euros, 5 million euros per year shared with measure 3.1.1. Success is measured by: Patients start treatment within the target times. Source: Implementation Roadmap 2026-2031, p. 56.

Barriers described in strategy texts (costs to patients, distance to the treating unit, health literacy, faster access through occupational health care) have no roadmap measure linked to them in this project.
