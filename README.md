# Junction 2026 – AstraZeneca Challenge

**Equal Care Across Finland**

## The Main Challenge: Equal Opportunities for Effective Cancer Treatment for Everyone in Finland

**The central challenge is to ensure that everyone in Finland receives timely, medically appropriate cancer diagnostics and treatment, regardless of where they live, their income, education level, employment status, or access to the healthcare system.**

Finland has a generally high-performing healthcare system. Nevertheless, significant regional and socioeconomic disparities persist in early detection, diagnosis, treatment, and survival among cancer patients.

AstraZeneca is therefore looking for a **nationwide, AI-powered solution** that not only makes these inequalities visible but also enables concrete, scientifically grounded improvements.

## 1. The Underlying Problem

The studies and presentations provided highlight four interconnected challenges.

### Regional Inequality

Healthcare in Finland is organized regionally. Differences in healthcare capacity, accessibility, staffing, and care pathways may mean that patients receive care at different speeds depending on where they live.

### Socioeconomic Inequality

People with lower incomes or lower levels of education experience poorer treatment outcomes on average. The national strategy identifies financial burdens, differences in health literacy, and barriers to access among the potential contributing factors.

### Delays Along the Care Pathway

Delays occur between the onset of symptoms, initial contact with a physician, referral, diagnosis, and the actual start of treatment. These delays may accumulate across several stages.

### Fragmented and Incomplete Data

Information on waiting times, care pathways, quality of care, and regional disparities is not equally available or comparable everywhere. This makes it difficult to reliably identify bottlenecks and assess improvement measures.

The Finnish implementation roadmap confirms both regional and socioeconomic disparities and challenges arising from parallel routes of access to healthcare.

**Source:** [Finland's National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035 – Implementation Roadmap 2026–2031](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_toimeenpanon_tiekartta_2026_2031.pdf), especially pp. 9–13.

## 2. AstraZeneca's Three Specific Tasks

1. **Identify the Gap – Detect Inequalities**

   Using reliable data, the AI should identify differences in access to cancer care or treatment outcomes.

2. **Locate the Gap – Pinpoint Where Differences Arise Along the Care Pathway**

   The solution should show where disparities emerge: during early detection, referral from primary to specialist care, diagnosis, or treatment initiation.

3. **Recommend What to Do – Propose Concrete Solutions**

   This is explicitly the most important part of the challenge. The AI should recommend transparent, scientifically evidence-based measures that can be implemented nationally or regionally and whose effectiveness can be evaluated.

AstraZeneca does not merely want to know **where the problems are**, but, more importantly, **what decision-makers can actually do about them**.

## 3. Why This Challenge Matters

| Cancer Care Costs | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Annual cancer care costs in 2023 | **€1.5 billion** |
| Projected annual cancer care costs in 2035 without specific interventions | **€2.6 billion** |

The national cancer strategy projects a substantial increase in healthcare costs. At the same time, population ageing is increasing the number of people who require cancer treatment.

**Source:** [Finland's National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf), especially pp. 6–8.

The challenge therefore connects three national priorities: better treatment outcomes, fairer access, and the responsible use of limited healthcare resources.

## 4. What Requirements Must the Solution Meet?

| Requirement | Expected Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Artificial intelligence | A functioning AI-powered analysis system |
| Nationwide applicability | A solution that can, in principle, be used across Finland |
| Scientific evidence | Traceable sources supporting every key claim |
| National Cancer Strategy Goal 3 | At least one relevant indicator |
| Concrete measures | Actionable recommendations for improvement |
| Data protection | Public, aggregate data only for the challenge |
| Working demo | A functional prototype rather than slides alone |
| Clarity | Decision-makers can understand the findings in under two minutes |

For **Goal 3**, it is especially relevant that patients should be able to start treatment within established target timeframes.

**Source:** [Finland's National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035](https://files.hackjunction.app/resources/Kansallinen_syopastrategia_2026_2035_FINAL.pdf), p. 5.

Using public, aggregate data only makes privacy-conscious development easier. However, full compliance with the GDPR must still be assessed against the actual system implementation.

## 5. The Challenge in a Nutshell

### The Core Challenge

*How can artificial intelligence help Finland identify inequalities in cancer care, pinpoint where delays and disparities arise, and recommend evidence-based interventions that ensure timely, equitable and effective treatment for everyone?*

In other words:

How can artificial intelligence help Finland reliably detect inequalities in cancer care, locate where they arise along the care pathway, and recommend scientifically grounded measures so that everyone receives timely, appropriate treatment regardless of their personal circumstances or region?

## 6. What Makes a Successful Submission?

The central success factor is **turning fragmented healthcare data into a clear, actionable basis for decision-making**.

A successful system should enable a decision-maker to answer the following questions within two minutes:

- Where is there a demonstrable inequality?
- At which point in the care pathway does it occur?
- Which causes are supported by evidence, and which remain uncertain?
- Which concrete measure should be evaluated or implemented?
- How can its effectiveness be measured afterwards?

The AI must not draw unsupported medical conclusions or independently make treatment decisions for individual people.

**In summary:** AstraZeneca is not looking for an AI that cures cancer or makes diagnoses. It is looking for a solution that helps Finland's healthcare system make existing cancer care **fairer, faster, more effective, and easier to manage**.

This is precisely where *Cancer Equity Intelligence* offers an opportunity: by combining evidence-based analysis, national strategic objectives, and concrete recommendations into a functional decision-support system.
