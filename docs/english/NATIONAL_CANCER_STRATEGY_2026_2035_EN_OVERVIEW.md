# Finland's National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035

## Together Against Cancer — Human-Centred Cancer Prevention and Care

## Summary (from original pages 3–4)

Finland's cancer burden will grow substantially in the next ten years. Population ageing is increasing the need for cancer treatment. More people are living with cancer or having survived it, and cancer and its treatments significantly affect quality of life and functional ability.

The strategy seeks to improve equity and quality of life for people affected by cancer, while supporting prevention and reducing cancer deaths. Cancer is not one disease but hundreds of different diseases, requiring cooperation across primary and specialist care, research, rehabilitation, and psychosocial services. These services should be available equitably and without unnecessary delay across the country.

The strategy was developed with broad expert participation, coordinated by the Finnish National Cancer Center (FICAN), together with the Ministry of Social Affairs and Health (STM), THL, and cancer organisations. Its implementation is accompanied by national monitoring and a separate implementation roadmap.

## Four strategic goals (original page 5)

### Goal 1 — Strengthen participation and develop human-centred services

- Systematically measure and report patient experience (Net Promoter Score, NPS), quality of life, and patient-reported outcomes (PROMs).
- Target NPS above 80 in units treating cancer patients.

### Goal 2 — Reduce the cancer burden through prevention and early detection

- Reduce the number of preventable cancers by **1,000 cases per year**.
- Reach **80% screening participation in every wellbeing services county** across all cancer screening programmes.
- Diagnose at least **70% of cancers at stages I–II**.

### Goal 3 — Ensure equitable and effective cancer treatment

- Raise five-year survival for poor-prognosis cancers to the Nordic comparison level.
- Reduce cancer mortality by **10%** (the source mentions approximately **13,300 cancer deaths annually** as the then-current baseline).
- Ensure that patients receive treatment **within target timeframes**.

The source specifies Nordic benchmarks for lung, pancreatic, and liver cancer (see its page 5 footnote). These targets must not be applied indiscriminately to every cancer type.

### Goal 4 — Stay at the forefront of a changing environment

- Increase the number of new clinical cancer studies and registry studies by **10% annually**.
- Achieve participation in intervention studies by more than **10% of cancer patients**.
- Establish a unified process for evaluating medicines used in cancer treatment.

### Cross-cutting priority — Equity

Make data on **regional and socioeconomic differences** in cancer incidence, treatment, and mortality available to service organisations and use it to guide action.

## Finland's cancer situation (original pages 6–9)

The strategy forecasts an increase in annual new cancer cases from just under **38,000** to nearly **45,000 by 2035** without further measures. By 2035, approximately **480,000 people** are expected to be living with or beyond cancer. Demographic ageing is a major contributor.

Cancer-care spending could rise from approximately **EUR 1.5 billion (2023)** to **EUR 2.6 billion (2035)** without targeted action. The document also notes socioeconomic and geographical differences in cancer incidence and mortality, and identifies prevention, equitable early detection, and timely access as areas requiring improvement.

The Finnish cancer landscape spans public authorities, wellbeing services counties, hospitals, FICAN, THL, the Cancer Registry, universities, civic organisations, and private-sector actors. This distributed structure challenges coordination, resource allocation and implementation of innovation.

## Equity in the strategy (original pages 11–12)

Formal equality — treating everyone in exactly the same way — is not sufficient to guarantee **substantive equity**. Individuals' circumstances, support needs, health literacy and opportunities to access appropriate services must be considered.

The six cross-cutting equity perspectives are:

1. Socioeconomic factors
2. Children and young people
3. Rare cancers and hereditary cancer predisposition syndromes
4. Regional inequality
5. Older people
6. Poor-prognosis cancers

## Sub-goals and measure areas (original pages 13–14)

### 1. Participation and human-centred services

- **1.1** Patients, service users and family members participate in developing cancer prevention, treatment and services.
- **1.2** Respectful, humane and individualised interactions help patients participate in their own care.
- **1.3** Clear and accessible health communication and health literacy support prevention, early detection and treatment effectiveness.

### 2. Prevention and early detection

- **2.1** Reduce cancer risk factors and strengthen protective factors through public policy.
- **2.2** Prevent cancer in wellbeing services counties and local services.
- **2.3** Use European recommendations and national structures to guide effective screening programmes.
- **2.4** Use legislation and registry data to support high-quality, cost-effective and risk-based screening.
- **2.5** Detect cancers early throughout healthcare.

### 3. Equitable and effective treatment

- **3.1** National treatment recommendations and care pathways guide equitable, timely and effective care.
- **3.2** A national cancer-care quality registry, systematic outcomes review and peer assessment support care improvement.
- **3.3** Rehabilitation, psychosocial support, healthcare social work and palliative care are established and equitably available parts of the care pathway.
- **3.4** Functional ability information informs cancer diagnosis and treatment for older patients.
- **3.5** Sufficient skilled staff ensure effective and human-centred cancer care.

### 4. Adapting to a changing environment

- **4.1** National health data resources and advanced analytics support cancer research and evidence use.
- **4.2** Clinical cancer research becomes an integral part of care.
- **4.3** Finland leads in personalised medicine.
- **4.4** Reduce cancer burden through multidisciplinary research.
- **4.5** Evaluate and introduce new cancer medicines and treatment methods effectively, promptly and consistently.

## Relevance to Equal Care Navigator Across Finland

These are **project interpretations**, not translated strategy directives:

- Citizen-facing accessible prevention and navigation support: goals **1.3, 2.3, 2.5**.
- Cross-region pathway and access analysis: goals **3.1, 3.2** and cross-cutting equity.
- Source-grounded analytics and trustworthy decision support: goal **4.1**.
- Prioritise transparent evidence, feasibility, and human oversight. Do not infer individual clinical eligibility from regional aggregate data.
